The Rosewood community engagement report from the 2026 surveys is up on Shapeyouripswich.com.au. One of the questions early on in the survey is "Which of these physical improvements would make the biggest difference to Rosewood in the future?". The answer that stands head and shoulders above the rest is "Safe paths and connections for bikes, mobility scooters and walking" at 51% of respondants. 

Who would have thought that bike paths and foot paths would be the thing that people rate as having the biggest impact?

It makes me wonder: are people elsewhere in Ipswich all that different? Why aren't people shouting at the council to build more bike lanes like they shout at the council to fix the pot-holes? It's clearly popular. There's sure to be votes in it. So why doesn't ICC pull up their collective socks and get building?

Maybe one answer relates to motonormativty bias. Described as "... a shared bias whereby people judge motorised mobility differently to other comparable topics." 

[Science Direct](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0959378025000172)

This research discovered that people incorrectly assume that very few others want non-car travel. "For example, 66% of USA respondents thought they supported non-car travel more than other local people" and "This lack of awareness of what other people think about non-car mobility might help explain public resistance to attempts to promote it."

I'd be very interested to see this research repeated in Australia but I suspect we aren't that different from the USA. Local councils and transport authorities should take note.
